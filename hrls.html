The Core Idea
Clinical decision support requires a difficult balance: the accuracy of deep learning with the transparency of linear models[cite: 16]. HRLS (Hierarchical Residual Logistic Sequences) resolves this by framing a temporal LSTM as a residual corrector to a static Logistic Regression baseline[cite: 7]. This ensures that the model remains grounded in interpretable features while capturing complex disease progression patterns[cite: 28].
Approach
We utilize a learnable parameter σ(α) to explicitly weight the influence of temporal dynamics. This allows clinicians to see exactly how much the model is "correcting" the baseline based on symptom timing[cite: 61, 64].
\[ z_T = LR_{logits} + \sigma(\alpha) \cdot RES_{logits} \]
To manage the scale of 721 diseases, we implement a two-stage hierarchy[cite: 29, 98, 114]. The first stage predicts broad disease categories, which then informs the fine-grained final diagnosis[cite: 99].
Performance
HRLS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on large-scale clinical data (246,823 samples) while maintaining a fraction of the complexity of "black-box" architectures[cite: 114].

Validation Insight: Our Temporal Explanation Robustness (TER) metric yielded a correlation of ρ = 0.481 (p < 0.001), confirming that the model's explanations are statistically stable and trustworthy[cite: 144].

Implications
By transitioning from heuristic-driven models to a principled residual architecture, HRLS demonstrates:
  • Scalability: Successfully handles over 700 disease classes across 25 categories[cite: 114].
  • Structural Transparency: Unlike post-hoc explainers, HRLS is interpretable by design through its residual weighting[cite: 38].
  • Early Detection: Achieves 66.8% accuracy as early as the 4th symptom timestep, enabling proactive clinical intervention[cite: 139].
