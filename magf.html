The Core Idea
Learned attention weights are often uninterpretable "black boxes." We replace them with a parameter-free mechanism grounded in data reliability. By weighting modalities based on their statistical stability—the Coefficient of Variation (CV)—we ensure that reliable clinical signals naturally guide the model's decision-making.
Approach
Traditional attention relies on optimization-heavy \( Softmax(QK^T) \). For high-stakes clinical fusion, we propose that importance should be proportional to statistical stability.
\[ \alpha_i = \frac{R_i}{\sum R_j} \quad \text{where} \quad R = \frac{|\mu|}{\sigma + \epsilon} \]
This relationship ensures that as a modality's relative variation decreases, its influence on the final diagnosis increases, making the model’s "trust" fully verifiable by a clinician.

Ablation Study: Removing the statistical weighting leads to a 34.1% performance drop, confirming that the CV-grounded logic is the primary driver of accuracy.

Implications
This work bridges the gap between heuristic-based ML and principled architectural design. By utilizing a deterministic statistical function in place of learned parameters, we achieved:
  • Reduced Complexity: ~60% fewer parameters compared to standard Transformers.
  • Inherent Trust: A fully interpretable fusion layer grounded in input statistics.
  • Efficiency: Faster inference by removing heavy attention computations.
