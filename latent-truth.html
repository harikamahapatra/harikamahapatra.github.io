The Latent Truth: Knowledge–Behavior Gap in Logical Deduction via Activation Steering

Aligning model outputs with latent reasoning representations
Vinayak Raj Urs, Harika Mahapatra
Preprint
Code (WIP) Paper
Language models often encode correct reasoning internally but fail to express it during generation. Activation steering aligns outputs with latent representations, recovering correct logical deductions without modifying model weights.
The Core Idea
Large language models frequently contain correct reasoning internally but fail to express it during generation. This discrepancy reveals a gap between what models know and what they produce. Rather than treating failures as missing knowledge, we investigate how reasoning is represented internally and where it breaks during decoding.
Key Insight
We find that models often encode correct logical structure in intermediate activations, but this structure is not faithfully reflected in the final output. This suggests that many failures arise from the generation process itself rather than limitations in representation.
Key Ideas
  • Correct reasoning is often present in intermediate representations
  • Failures emerge during decoding rather than knowledge formation
  • Targeted activation interventions can recover correct outputs
Method
We probe intermediate activations across layers to identify signals corresponding to correct reasoning. Using these signals, we apply activation steering during generation, guiding the model toward outputs that better align with its latent structure.

This approach operates entirely at inference time and does not require retraining, fine-tuning, or modification of model weights. It provides a lightweight and general mechanism for improving reasoning consistency across tasks.
Results
Activation steering recovers a significant portion of logical failures while preserving fluency and coherence. Our findings suggest that model errors often stem from misaligned decoding rather than insufficient knowledge, highlighting a new direction for improving reliability in language models.
