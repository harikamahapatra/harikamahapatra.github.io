The Latent Truth: Knowledge–Behavior Gap in Logical Deduction via Activation Steering

Aligning model outputs with latent reasoning representations
Large language models often encode correct logical reasoning internally but fail to express it during generation. We identify this as a knowledge–behavior gap and show that activation steering acts as a causal intervention, recovering ~75% of logical failures by aligning outputs with latent representations — without retraining.

Core Idea
Large language models frequently contain correct reasoning internally but fail to express it during generation. This reveals a gap between what models know and what they produce. Rather than treating failures as missing knowledge, we analyze how reasoning is represented internally and where it breaks during decoding.
Key Insight
Models encode logically correct structure in intermediate activations, but this structure is not faithfully reflected in final outputs. This suggests failures arise from the decoding process rather than representational limitations.
Method
We probe intermediate activations across layers to identify signals corresponding to correct reasoning. Using these signals, we apply activation steering during generation, introducing targeted perturbations in representation space.

This functions as a causal intervention, testing whether latent representations are not only present but functionally sufficient to drive correct outputs.

The approach operates entirely at inference time and requires no retraining or modification of model weights, providing a lightweight mechanism for improving reasoning reliability.
Cross-Model Validation
We extend our analysis to Mistral 7B to test whether the knowledge–behavior gap is architecture-specific.
  • Intermediate representations achieve high logical accuracy across layers
  • Behavioral accuracy remains significantly lower, confirming the persistence of the gap
  • Activation steering improves performance with a clear optimal intervention range (α ≈ 1.0)
These results demonstrate that reasoning failures are not due to missing knowledge, but arise during decoding — and can be consistently corrected via targeted interventions.
Results
Activation steering improves reasoning accuracy from ~65% to ~75% by recovering a large fraction of logical failures while preserving fluency and coherence. These findings indicate that model errors stem from misaligned decoding rather than insufficient knowledge, highlighting a new direction for improving reliability in language models.
