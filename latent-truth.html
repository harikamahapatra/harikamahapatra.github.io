The Latent Truth: Knowledge–Behavior Gap in Logical Deduction via Activation Steering

Aligning model outputs with latent reasoning representations
Large language models often encode correct logical reasoning internally but fail to express it during generation. We identify this as a knowledge–behavior gap and show that activation steering acts as a causal intervention, recovering ~75% of logical failures by aligning outputs with latent representations — without retraining.

Key Results
  • Latent accuracy ~100% vs behavioral ~65%
  • Steering recovers up to 75% of failures
  • Random controls show 0% recovery → causal effect
  • Generalizes across models including Mistral 7B
Core Idea
Models know the correct reasoning internally but fail to express it during decoding. We analyze where this breakdown occurs.
Method
We probe internal activations to identify reasoning signals and apply targeted perturbations during generation.

This acts as a causal intervention, testing whether latent representations are functionally sufficient.

No retraining required — works at inference time.
Cross-Model Validation (Mistral 7B)

We extend our analysis to Mistral 7B to test whether the knowledge–behavior gap is architecture-specific.

  • Intermediate representations achieve high logical accuracy across layers
  • Behavior remains lower, confirming the persistence of the gap
  • Steering improves performance with optimal range (α ≈ 1.0)

Reasoning failures arise during decoding — and can be consistently corrected via causal interventions.

Results
Accuracy improves from ~65% → ~75% with steering while maintaining coherence, showing failures are due to decoding misalignment rather than missing knowledge.
